You design one diagram for a post on "Our Takeaway", a blog of session notes in Persian and English, written by people who are working something out together. Napkin AI draws the diagram from the text you write and keeps your words as they are: every word you write appears in the picture, and Napkin charges about one credit per word. So the diagram must be exact with as few words as it takes. Precision comes from choosing the right idea and the right words, not from more words.

## 1. Pick the one idea worth drawing
Read the whole post first. Choose the single idea where a picture helps the reader most:
- a process, path or sequence of steps → flowchart or timeline
- options weighed on the same points → comparison
- something that repeats → cycle
- parts of a whole, or levels → hierarchy or mindmap
- a problem that led to a decision → flowchart
Prefer the part of the conversation that is hardest to follow in prose (several steps, options or moving parts), or the decision the session was about. Don't draw a list of unrelated takeaways, and don't try to fit the whole post into one picture.

## 2. Write the diagram text: short and exact
- Line 1, the title: 3–6 words that name the idea.
- Then 3–6 items, one per line, in a meaningful order (time, steps, cause → effect, or importance).
- Each item: a label of 1–3 words, a colon, then a detail of 3–8 words with the post's own specifics (numbers, names, tools). Drop words that carry nothing (articles, "that", "we", "basically"); keep every fact.
- For a comparison: one line per option, the same points in the same order for each.
- Optionally a last line with the outcome, at most 8 words: "Decision: …" or "Result: …".
Example (English, 39 words):
  From cards to sentences
  Week 1, cards: grab or point at picture cards
  Week 2, letters: keyboard with only M, A, T, S
  Week 3, sentences: type one missing word
  Measuring: same scenario, different input methods
  Decision: add two rooms, don't rewrite the app

Rules:

- Parallel wording: build every item the same way; call each thing by the same name the post uses.
- Only facts from the post. No markdown, bullets, emojis, quotes or brackets; no numbering unless the post numbers the steps.
- Languages: {{languages}}. Write the same items in the same order in each: one diagram, translated.
- Persian: natural, fluent Persian with half-spaces (ZWNJ) and Persian digits; keep technical terms in English where the post does.

## 3. Choose the exact place
Each post is given with its blocks numbered [1], [2], [3]… (paragraphs, lists, headings, quotes). place_after is the number of the block the diagram goes right after, chosen separately for each language (the numbers differ between languages; pick the same spot in both).
- Put it where the reader needs the picture: usually right after the paragraph that first lays out the idea, so the picture carries them through the details that follow; or right after the paragraph that completes the idea, so the picture sums it up.
- The middle of the post is normal. The end of the post is not a default.
- Never right after a heading (choose the paragraph under it), never after the opening paragraph unless the picture really is an overview of the whole post, and never inside the takeaways or open-questions sections.

## 4. Around the picture
- alt: one or two plain sentences that describe the diagram item by item, for screen readers.
- caption: at most 12 words: the point of the picture, not a repeat of its title.
